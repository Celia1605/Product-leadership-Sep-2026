> Module 6 · Leverage AI Tools for Leadership Execution
>
> Your personal reflection on building the strategy end to end.

## Friction points

_What was the hardest part of building this strategy, and how did you work through it?_

> Honestly, the hardest part was the financials. I've never owned a P&L, and I'll admit I still barely understand it — things like CAC, LTV and payback didn't come naturally, and I know it'll take me a while to get comfortable managing all the data a product throws at you. I worked through it by slowing down, asking a lot of "what does this number actually mean" questions, and focusing on the one or two figures that really drove the decision instead of trying to grasp everything at once. The other hard part was resisting the urge to make the strategy sound more certain than it was. It was tempting to write things as if they were proven when they were really just bets, and I had to keep catching myself and being honest about what I actually knew versus what I was hoping.

## Key learnings

_A few surprising discoveries or insights you gained from the course overall._

> The biggest shift for me was how I think about stakeholders. I used to approach them fairly flatly — now I see that every stakeholder is protecting something specific, and if you don't figure out what that is, you're negotiating blind. Understanding their real interest (not just what they're asking for) changes the whole conversation. I also learned there are different levels of leverage, and knowing which one you're holding matters — like realising I only needed something for 8 weeks but asked for the full quarter, so those extra weeks became something I could trade instead of give away. And saying "no" well turned out to be a skill in itself: a hard no lands much better when you acknowledge what it costs the other person and give them a real path forward, instead of just blocking them.

## "Aha!" moment

_Your main "aha" moment during the project process._

> My "aha" was realising that managing stakeholders and priorities isn't about winning the argument — it's about understanding what each person actually needs and being honest about what's a real commitment versus a bet. Once I started leading with the other person's interest and being precise about what I could and couldn't promise, the hard conversations got easier. That felt like a genuinely different way to lead than how I'd done it before.
