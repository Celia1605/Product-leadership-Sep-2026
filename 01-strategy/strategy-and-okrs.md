# Product Strategy One-Pager & OKRs, Module 1

- **Scenario:** Fable Growth (B2C)

## Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Choice |
|---|---|
| Winning aspiration | Fable is the one people reach for when the acute crisis has passed but they don't want to get blindsided by the next one — the app that stood by them at their worst and now helps them stay ahead of it. |
| Where to play | We focus on our own high-engagement users at the 60–120 day transition — the moment acute need fades — in core English-speaking markets, in-app, serving the off-ramp from crisis to steady-state. We explicitly do not outspend Calm/Headspace on top-of-funnel brand — but we keep acquisition running, since the retention play only compounds if new acute users keep entering the funnel. We do not serve clinical or diagnosable populations, do not build a general social feed, and do not treat "more content" as the strategy. (Note: no feed ≠ no social — see capabilities.) |
| How to win | We win if — and our central bet is that — the trust and behavioural history formed during the user's hardest moment is a latent asset rather than baggage. Our hypothesis, to be proven at the gate, is that the association repels under remedial framing but retains under aspirational framing ("who you're becoming," not "who you were"). On that basis, we alone can detect an early dip and intervene before the next crisis using skills the user already trusts. Competitors win on brand or novelty, but none were there for this user, and a new AI app has no track record with them. |
| Capabilities required | We must be world-class at the interpretation layer of early-signal detection — what a behavioural dip means for a Fable user specifically (build — the moat is our proprietary labelled data and domain reading) — and at behaviour-change/identity design (build/hire). We add light, private social accountability (small structured connection, not a public feed) as a durability lever, so retention rests on more than notifications. The commoditised anomaly-detection infrastructure we buy/partner for. We partner with clinical advisory for CBT-informed, strictly non-diagnostic "mental fitness" skills, build personalisation on our proprietary data rather than buy a generic engine, and keep the meditation/sleep foundation as-is. |
| Management systems | We track retention of the acute cohort past the day-120 cliff and the proactive-use ratio (maintenance vs. crisis engagement) on a weekly cohort-review cadence — with the "proactive" vs. "crisis-triggered" session definition locked and owned by analytics before the quarter starts, not by the team measured on it. We track paid retention of the post-acute cohort and run a quarterly kill-review of anything not moving the proactive-use ratio. |

## One hard no
We will not build out Fable into a diagnostic or clinical mental-health platform — even though the clinical team's CBT depth is real and the demand from distressed users is obvious — because the moment we cross into diagnosis and treatment we take on regulatory and liability exposure the business can't carry, we start competing on a field where we have no right to win, and we betray the exact thing that makes us trusted: Fable is the gentle place people go, not the clinical one, and a resilience strategy built on that trust dies the instant we medicalise it.

## OKR cascade
- **Objective:** Prove that acute-cohort users can be carried past the day-120 cliff into durable, proactive engagement — turning the retention crisis into evidence the resilience thesis works.
- **KR1:** Day-120 retention of the high-engagement acute cohort from 22% to 35% by Dec 31, 2026.
- **KR2:** Proactive-use ratio (share of sessions that are maintenance, not crisis-triggered) among post-acute users from 15% to 40% by Dec 31, 2026.
- **KR3:** Day-60 paid retention of post-acute cohorts entering after launch, from 60% to 72% by Dec 31, 2026 — a leading indicator; durable day-180 paid retention tracked as the 2027 lagging measure.
